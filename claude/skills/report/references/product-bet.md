# Product bet review

Use this when the decision is whether a product improves a specific user's outcome enough to justify adoption. Judge the result users receive before architecture or feature inventory.

1. Identify the user, triggering job, current substitute, and what would make switching worthwhile.
2. Inspect real usage, support or research evidence, and the product itself. Separate observed behavior from claims, plans, and market assumptions.
3. Compare the same job under comparable access, data, cost, time, and assistance constraints against credible substitutes, including manual work or no action.
4. Identify capabilities that improve the result and those that consume attention or maintenance without doing so.
5. Recommend keeping, deleting, simplifying, or rebuilding. Preserve the current design when it meets the requirement.
6. Name evidence that could reverse the decision and the smallest useful way to get it.

Read [product evidence questions](product-evidence.md) when adoption economics, repeat use, or agent behavior matter beyond a direct walkthrough. Use independent perspectives only when they expose distinct assumptions and delegation is authorized.

Report `ship`, `narrow`, `rebuild`, `kill`, or `insufficient evidence`: user, job, strongest substitute, evidence, material uncertainty, justified changes, and the user observation or comparison that would prove them. A prototype failure does not disprove the category; a favorable demo or feature count does not prove demand. Keep recommendations within the task's authority. When implementation is requested, complete the justified changes and checks.
