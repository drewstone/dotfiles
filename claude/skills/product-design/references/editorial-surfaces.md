# Editorial surfaces

Use the page's purpose to choose navigation and evidence presentation.
Read the repository's current editorial and design guidance before choosing a direction.

## Blog indexes and articles

Organize the archive by paths readers actually use: series, topic, date, author, or argument.
Filters should change the visible archive and preserve a usable empty state.
Use covers, screenshots, diagrams, or other artifacts when they help identify a piece or explain its argument.
A post count belongs only where the reader uses volume to choose or compare.

Match article layout to its content.
Code, tables, diagrams, and screenshots should explain the argument; adding an artifact merely to satisfy a quota creates decoration.
Avoid implying that every product essay or implementation note is original research.

## Research indexes

Expose the claim, method, evidence, date, and limitations needed to assess the work.
Distinguish original results from comparisons, explainers, and product announcements.
Organize navigation around arguments and evidence standards that the actual collection supports.
Make sources and artifacts inspectable without requiring the reader to understand internal tool names.

## Choosing references

Inspect current references for the decision being made rather than copying a brand's whole visual style.
For example, an engineering article can inform diagram placement, while a changelog can inform a compact chronological archive.
Prefer useful patterns from the repository's existing pages and versions the user liked before introducing a new direction.
Verify the resulting navigation, text fit, and artifact presentation in the browser.
