# Product evidence questions

Use the relevant section when a direct walkthrough cannot answer the product decision.
State what was observed and what remains an assumption.

## Adoption and economics

- Who experiences the problem, who chooses the tool, and who pays?
- What substitute do they actually use under time pressure, including doing nothing?
- Which migration, procurement, training, integration, or trust costs could outweigh the product improvement?
- What does repeat use show about the claimed benefit? Distinguish stated enthusiasm from observed adoption.
- Can the proposed price cover measured delivery, support, and operating costs under realistic use?
- What evidence supports the path to reaching users and retaining them?

Use existing interviews, support records, usage data, and financial evidence within the task's authority.
An audit does not authorize contacting customers or running a pricing experiment.
Do not infer a market-wide verdict from an unrepresentative demo or interview.

## Experience beyond the first result

Trace how the user starts, reaches a useful result, inspects it, changes direction, recovers from failure, and uses it elsewhere.
Choose moments that matter to this product; a fixed onboarding sequence is not a universal requirement.

For comparative tests, use equivalent jobs, inputs, resources, and completion criteria where possible.
Keep the raw outputs and disclose material asymmetries.
Compare quality, time, cost, trust, and user control when those dimensions affect the decision.
Do not treat a faster incomplete result as equivalent to a completed task.

## Agent products

Inspect transcripts and generated artifacts alongside the actions that produced them.
Follow claims about tools, research, delegation, memory, recovery, and external effects through to an observed user result.
Check whether stopping, steering, retrying, and resuming preserve the user's task and avoid duplicate effects where those capabilities are promised.

Compare the complete workflow with a credible general assistant or the user's existing process under comparable constraints.
A particular architecture, tool count, or visible delegation is not required for value.
If an internal benchmark favors the product while users prefer its substitute, inspect both the benchmark's coverage and the raw user experience.
